Social Media Analytics

Undergraduate course, 3 credits (3 SKS) · 14 weekly meetings · Informatics study program

Lecturer: Dr. Halim Maulana, S.T., M.Kom.

About this course

Social media is where a large part of public life now takes place. People sell, argue, grieze, celebrate, organize, and ask for help in spaces that were never built for research. This course teaches students how to read those spaces honestly: how to collect data through paths that platforms allow, how to clean it without quietly distorting the meaning, and how to turn millions of small voices into a clear and defensible account of what people actually think and do.

We hold one belief throughout the fourteen weeks. Data collected from people should be treated with the same care we would want for our own words. Every method in this course comes with a question attached: who is represented, who is missed, and what does this dataset honestly allow us to say. A graduate who can answer that question is more useful to society than one who only knows how to run a script.

What students will be able to do

By the end of the course, students can choose a social media platform and justify that choice on technical, legal, and ethical grounds; collect data through official channels while keeping a reproducible collection log; clean messy multilingual text without destroying its meaning; run analyses that range from text mining to network analysis; evaluate the reliability of labels produced with the help of large language models; and present findings to an audience that includes non specialists.

Course modules

Each module below is a complete teaching book: narrative explanation, tables, figures, worked examples, glossary, and references. Open any of them in a browser on a phone or a computer.

Module 1: Foundations of Social Media Analytics

The data cycle, levels of analytic maturity, the difference between a number and an insight, and why the unit of analysis must be chosen before data collection starts.

Open Module 1

Module 2: Platform Ecosystems and Data Affordance

What each platform actually allows us to see, how recommendation feeds shape the sample we collect, and the five rungs of access in 2026.

Open Module 2

Module 3: Data Types and the Field Dictionary

Five families of social media data, the anatomy of an API object, granularity, and the three kinds of missingness that mislead beginners.

Open Module 3

Module 4: Data Collection through Official APIs

Authentication, pagination, rate limits, backoff, checkpointing, checksums, and the collection log that makes a dataset reproducible.

Open Module 4

Module 5: Gated Access, Cost, Scraping, and Law

Research APIs, paid enclaves, the six checks that keep scraping on the right side of the line, and what personal data protection law means for a student with a small budget.

Open Module 5

Module 6: Preprocessing and Data Quality

A seven stage pipeline, near duplicate detection, bot heuristics, normalising mixed Indonesian and English text, and the trap of cleaning a sentence into its opposite meaning.

Open Module 6

Module 7: Summary of Modules 1 to 6 and the Midterm Blueprint

One map that connects all six modules, a concept summary per module, the full midterm blueprint with weights, and guided practice questions with answers.

Open Module 7

Independent assignment

Each student picks one platform that gives them the most accessible data, collects it themselves, and searches for as many provable findings as possible. The grade follows the findings, not the length of the report. Every finding must trace back to the student's own rows of data.
